The Best Sandbars Near Miami and Fort Lauderdale by Yacht, 2026

One thing applies everywhere: a sandbar is only a few feet deep, and how much of it you can stand on depends on the tide. That is why the captain, not the guest list, decides exactly where the boat anchors.

Haulover Sandbar

This is the one most people mean when they say “the sandbar” in Miami. Miami-Dade County’s own directions for Bill Bird Marina in Haulover Park warn boaters that “there is a sandbar between the ICW and the Haulover Inlet Channel”: a shallow flat on the bay side of the inlet, between Bal Harbour and Sunny Isles Beach. On a warm weekend afternoon it fills with boats rafted together, music and people standing waist-deep with drinks.

Getting there. Boats from North Miami and Aventura have the shortest run; it is also an easy trip up the bay from Miami Beach. See our Miami Beach page for boats based nearby.

The rules that matter. The sandbar sits right beside a county park, a marina and a busy boat ramp, so two setbacks apply. Florida law (s. 327.4109) bars anchoring within 150 feet of any marina, boat ramp or launching facility, measured from the closest point of the boat, swing included. Miami-Dade’s park rules also restrict mooring or anchoring within 200 feet of a county park’s shoreline without written permission. The inlet channel next to the flat carries boat traffic all day, so swim on the sand, never in the channel.

Best for: bachelorette and birthday groups who want energy. If that is your crowd, see our bachelorette and birthday pages.

Nixon Sandbar and the Mashta Flats, Key Biscayne

On the bay side of Key Biscayne, near the site of President Nixon’s former Key Biscayne compound that gives the sandbar its name, the Mashta Flats are Miami’s other big raft-up. The water is usually clearer than at Haulover, the skyline sits on the horizon, and on weekdays you may share it with only a few boats.

The rules here are stricter than visitors expect. After several serious accidents, the Village of Key Biscayne voted in 2015 to close the part of the Mashta Flats inside village limits to all vessels. The raft-up sits on state water outside that area. All of Biscayne Bay is also a state aquatic preserve, and under Florida law (s. 253.04) scarring seagrass with a propeller outside a marked channel is a boating citation. Captains anchor on bare white sand, not on the dark grass patches, and that is one more reason to go with someone who knows the flat.

Getting there. Boats that board at Rickenbacker Marina or elsewhere on Key Biscayne are minutes away; from Brickell and Downtown it is a short run across the bay. See Brickell and Downtown for boats on the mainland side.

Best for: families, couples and groups who want a swim more than a party. It also works well as the quiet first stop of a proposal charter before a sunset on the bay.

Stiltsville and the flats south of Key Biscayne

South of Key Biscayne, a handful of wooden houses stand on stilts above the bay’s shallow seagrass beds. This is Stiltsville, now inside the northernmost part of Biscayne National Park, about a mile off the coast. It is the most photogenic water in Miami and makes a great add-on to a Key Biscayne sandbar day.

Set expectations, though: this is a place to cruise past, not to climb onto. The National Park Service says the structures are open by permit only, through the Stiltsville Trust. The flats around them are very shallow and grassy, so boats keep to deeper water, and the park bans personal watercraft such as jet skis in its waters. NOAA chart 11451 covers the area, and the park itself stresses that its waters are shallow and tide predictions matter.

Fort Lauderdale and Boca Raton: Lake Sylvia and Lake Boca

Lake Sylvia, Fort Lauderdale. Fort Lauderdale’s go-to anchorage is a calm, enclosed lake off the Intracoastal in the Harbor Beach neighborhood, rather than a true sandbar. The city designates it a watersports activity area, so expect tubing and wakeboarding at the edges and boats anchored to swim and float in the middle. It is sheltered from the ocean, which makes it a good choice on a breezy winter day. One honest caution: Miami Waterkeeper samples Lake Sylvia every week, and its Swim Guide notes that samples taken within 48 hours of rain are likely to exceed bacteria standards. Check the latest result before you swim. The city also limits overnight anchoring in its waterways, which matters for liveaboards, not for a day charter.

Lake Boca, Boca Raton. Farther north, Lake Boca Raton has a popular sandbar just inside the Boca Raton Inlet, with the city’s public mooring field right beside it. The whole lake is a year-round slow-speed manatee zone under the state’s Palm Beach County rule, so boats move slowly and it is a relaxed hangout on most weekends. The exception is Boca Bash, the huge boat party held on a Sunday in late April (April 26 in 2026), when police close nearby parks and limit boat ramps. Unless that is the plan, pick another weekend.

Tides, crowds and the best time to go

  • Tide. South Florida has two high and two low tides most days, about six hours apart. NOAA’s long-term averages put the range between high and low at about 2.5 feet at Haulover and Port Everglades and about 2 feet at Virginia Key, next to Key Biscayne. On a flat that is only a few feet deep, that is the difference between wading and swimming, so ask your captain to time the stop.
  • Day of the week. Weekend afternoons and holiday weekends are the peak. Miami-Dade notes that on summer and holiday weekends, Haulover Park is busiest on Sundays from 11 a.m. to 3 p.m.; on the water, weekend afternoons are the busiest too. For space to swim, choose a weekday or arrive by late morning.
  • Season. Sandbar season runs from spring to fall, but winter works on warm, calm days, with shorter light: sunset in Miami is about 5:30 p.m. in early December and around 7:30 p.m. by mid-March, after daylight saving time starts. Plan a winter charter to start by midday. In winter some of Biscayne Bay’s manatee zones also switch to slow speed (November 15 to April 30 in several areas), so runs take a little longer.
  • Wind. A breezy day makes the flats choppy and cloudy. If the forecast is rough, a sheltered spot like Lake Sylvia or a cruise in the lee of an island is better, and your captain will suggest it.

What to bring: reef-safe sunscreen, water shoes, a layer for the ride back, and drinks in cans or plastic, since most yachts ask for no glass on board. There are no restrooms, food or trash bins on a sandbar, so everything comes back to the boat. And as on the road, Florida’s boating-under-the-influence limit is 0.08; with a hired captain, that is not your problem.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best sandbar in Miami?

It depends on the day you want. The Haulover Sandbar, just inside Haulover Inlet between Bal Harbour and Sunny Isles Beach, is the biggest party scene on weekends. The Nixon Sandbar on the bay side of Key Biscayne has clearer water and a big Sunday raft-up but is calmer on weekdays. For a sightseeing cruise rather than a party, the flats around Stiltsville in Biscayne National Park are the most scenic.

When is the best time to go to a Miami sandbar?

Plan around the tide and the day of the week. The water over a sandbar is shallowest around low tide and deepest around high tide, and the two change roughly every six hours, so ask your captain to time the stop for wading depth. Weekdays and weekend mornings are quieter; weekend afternoons and holiday weekends are the busiest.

Can a big yacht anchor on a sandbar?

Not on the shallowest part. Sandbars are only a few feet deep, so larger yachts anchor in deeper water at the edge and guests swim, float or take the tender in, while smaller boats can sit closer. Your captain decides where it is safe and legal to anchor.

Are there rules at the Nixon Sandbar in Key Biscayne?

Yes. The Village of Key Biscayne closed the part of the Mashta Flats inside village limits to vessels in 2015, and Biscayne Bay is a state aquatic preserve, where scarring seagrass with a propeller is a boating citation. Boats anchor on bare sand outside the closed area.

How much does a sandbar yacht charter cost in Miami?

Standard listed rates on latitude25.us start at $520 for 4 hours for up to 6 guests, $605 for up to 13, $805 for up to 18 and $1,440 for up to 25 guests, including captain and crew. Most sandbar days are booked for 4 to 6 hours. A 15 to 20 percent crew gratuity is customary and is usually separate.

Is there a sandbar in Fort Lauderdale?

Fort Lauderdale's version is Lake Sylvia, a calm, protected anchorage in the Harbor Beach neighborhood rather than a true sandbar, which the city designates as a watersports area. The best-known sandbar in the area is farther north at Lake Boca Raton, just inside the Boca Raton Inlet.
