Miami Art Week 2026 for First-Timers

More than twenty fairs, two cities and one bay in a single week. Here is how the 2026 fairs split between Miami Beach and the mainland, how to get between them without losing an afternoon in traffic, a simple three-day plan, and what Art Week looks like from Biscayne Bay.

The week at a glance

Miami Art Week 2026 runs Monday, November 30 to Sunday, December 6. The anchor is Art Basel Miami Beach at the Miami Beach Convention Center. Its VIP Preview Days, on December 2 and 3, are by invitation only. Its public days are Friday, December 4 to Sunday, December 6, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Most satellite fairs open on December 1 or 2, so if you arrive on Friday, part of the week is already behind you.

Art Week also happens in two places: Miami Beach, and the mainland across Biscayne Bay. Plan your days by side, not by fair.

Miami Beach: Art Basel and the walkable fairs

The Convention Center cluster. Art Basel brings 272 galleries from more than 40 countries to the Convention Center this year, on a reconfigured show floor. Design Miami, the collectible-design fair, sits right beside it in Pride Park, open to the public from Wednesday afternoon, December 2; it sells its own tickets. Nearby, the Bass Museum on Collins Avenue has a free community day on Sunday, December 6, from noon to 5 p.m., and the Public Art Fund’s Between Tides (artist-designed ping-pong tables) is in Collins Park next door.

The Ocean Drive strip. Ten minutes south, a second cluster lines up along the beach: Untitled Art in its tent on the sand at 12th Street, InScope at Ocean Drive in South Beach, and PRIZM at Ocean Drive in South Beach, with late hours (to 9 p.m. Thursday to Saturday). This end of the week rewards walking: two fairs, one street, no car.

Free fillers. The city’s No Vacancy program puts works by 12 artists in 12 Miami Beach hotel lobbies through December 19, and the Wolfsonian-FIU on Washington Avenue has free Art Week admission.

The mainland: Downtown, Wynwood and the Design District

Downtown, on the bay. Art Miami, the oldest fair of the week, and its sister fair CONTEXT share a pavilion at One Herald Plaza, on the water between the Venetian and MacArthur causeways. It opens to the public Tuesday, December 1, at 4 p.m.; tickets are sold online only. A few minutes south on Biscayne Boulevard, the Pérez Art Museum Miami (PAMM) has a bayfront terrace and, this season, Basquiat: Figures, Signs, Symbols and Picasso–Klee–Matisse.

Wynwood and the edge of it. NADA Miami, where you meet emerging galleries before everyone else does, is at Ice Palace Studios, 1400 North Miami Avenue, with more than 130 galleries. It runs December 1 to 5 and closes a day before the others, so do it early. Wynwood’s murals and galleries are a short ride north.

The Design District and Allapattah. The Institute of Contemporary Art, Miami (ICA) at 61 NE 41st Street is free, Wednesday to Sunday, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m.; reserve a timed ticket in advance. West of Wynwood, the Rubell Museum in Allapattah is open Wednesday to Sunday.

A simple three-day plan

Nobody sees all of it. Pick one side per day and accept that you will skip things.

  • Day 1, mainland, midweek (Wednesday or Thursday). NADA in the morning, Art Miami and CONTEXT after lunch, then PAMM or the ICA. You beat the weekend crowds, and you see NADA before it closes on Saturday.
  • Day 2, Art Basel (Friday). Go early on the first public day, give the main fair four or five hours, then cross the street to Design Miami. Eat before you go in.
  • Day 3, the beach (Saturday or Sunday). Untitled on the sand in the morning, the beach at midday, InScope or Aqua in the late afternoon, then dinner on the beach side so you are not crossing a causeway at 6 p.m.

Book dinners now. Art Week is one of the busiest restaurant weeks of the year in Miami Beach, and the good tables near the Convention Center go first.

Getting around without the causeway crawl

The City of Miami Beach says the heaviest Art Week traffic is on weekday afternoons, building toward the weekend, and that westbound traffic on the MacArthur Causeway will be slowed again this year by roadwork near the Biscayne Boulevard off-ramp. The free options are better than they sound:

  • The free water taxi crosses Biscayne Bay every 7 to 9 minutes between Maurice Gibb Memorial Park (Purdy Avenue near Sunset Harbour, Sunset Harbour) and the Venetian Marina & Yacht Club (1635 North Bayshore Drive, near the Omni garage). It runs 10 a.m. to midnight from November 30 to December 5, and until 10 p.m. on December 6. A shuttle links the Miami Beach stop to the Convention Center (or walk it in 20 to 25 minutes).
  • Free Art Week shuttles run every 10 to 15 minutes from Convention Center Drive and 18th Street: south to Collins Avenue at 10th–11th streets (the Ocean Drive fairs), north to Mid Beach, and across the Julia Tuttle Causeway to the Design District (NE 38th Street).
  • The free Miami Beach Trolley runs more often, 8 a.m. to midnight, from December 2 to 5.
  • Rideshare for the Convention Center and Design Miami drops at 19th Street and Meridian Avenue, or on Convention Center Drive between 18th and 19th streets.

Our honest advice: base yourself on one side, use the water taxi to cross once a day, and do not plan two fairs on opposite sides of the bay in the same afternoon.

Seeing Art Week from the water

You cannot see the fairs themselves from a boat; they are indoors or in tents. What you get is the Downtown skyline lit up behind Biscayne Bay, the Art Miami pavilion and PAMM on the bayfront, the mansions of Star Island, and Miami Beach seen from the bay side. Sunset in early December is around 5:30 p.m., right as the fairs wind down, which is why a 4-hour evening outing is the most popular way to use a boat that week.

Three practical notes. First, choose your boarding side to match your day: yachts in Miami Beach board minutes from the Convention Center, while Downtown and Brickell boats suit a mainland day. Second, the East Venetian Causeway drawbridge will not open for boats from 3 to 9 p.m. every day of Art Week, November 30 to December 6, so boats too tall to pass under it take another route; your captain plans for this. Frequently asked questions

When is Miami Art Week 2026?

Monday, November 30 to Sunday, December 6, 2026. Art Basel Miami Beach is open to the public December 4 to 6, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m., after invitation-only VIP days on December 2 and 3. Most satellite fairs open December 1 or 2.

Which Art Week fairs are on Miami Beach and which are in Miami?

Miami Beach has Art Basel, Design Miami, Untitled Art, InScope and Aqua. The mainland has Art Miami and CONTEXT in Downtown Miami and NADA Miami at Ice Palace Studios near Wynwood.

Is there free transportation during Miami Art Week?

Yes. A free water taxi crosses Biscayne Bay every 7 to 9 minutes between Maurice Gibb Memorial Park in Miami Beach and the Venetian Marina in Miami, from November 30 to December 6, and free shuttles link it to the fairs and the Design District.

Can you see Art Basel from a boat?

Not the fair itself, which is indoors. From Biscayne Bay you see the Downtown skyline, the Art Miami pavilion and PAMM on the bayfront, and Miami Beach from the bay side, which makes a sunset cruise a popular break after the fairs close.
