When winter viewing is actually good

Florida’s manatees seek warm water when the weather turns. That is why winter, and January in particular, is the right season, and also why a random date in January can still be quiet. The animals gather in warm-water outflows, springs and sheltered canals after a few cold days in a row. A mild, cloudy week can mean fewer animals at a given site, even in season.

Manatee Lagoon, the free viewing center in West Palm Beach, puts it plainly: season there runs from November 15 through March 31, the best looks are after two or three cool days with less cloud cover, and there is never a guarantee. Build a day that is still pleasant if you see only a few animals, or none, and check conditions the morning you drive.

Manatee Lagoon in West Palm Beach

If you have a car and want the most reliable nearby option, start here. Manatee Lagoon – An FPL Eco-Discovery Center is a free educational center on the Lake Worth Lagoon in West Palm Beach, about an hour and a half north of Miami, depending on traffic. On cold days, manatees often gather in the warm-water outflow from the Florida Power & Light plant next door, sometimes in large groups, in view of the upstairs observation deck.

What to know before you go, from the center’s own visitor page as of November 13, 2026:

  • Admission and parking are free. No reservation is required for a regular visit.
  • Hours are 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. During manatee season (November 15 through March 31) the center is open seven days a week. The rest of the year it is closed on Mondays.
  • It is closed on New Year’s Day, Easter, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas, and it closes at 2 p.m. on Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve. It is open on Martin Luther King Jr. Day, which matters if your mid-January trip includes that Monday.
  • The upstairs space and observation deck sometimes close for private events, weather or maintenance. The center posts those dates on its site and updates them monthly, so check the morning you go.
  • There is a live manatee cam, with above-water and underwater views, on the Manatee Lagoon site and through EarthCam. Watch it before you leave Miami.

Details, directions and the cam are at visitmanateelagoon.com. There is no swimming or fishing at the center, and pets are not allowed (service animals are). The indoor exhibits are worth the stop even on a slow wildlife day.

Kayaks and paddleboards near Miami

Manatees do show up in Biscayne Bay, in its canals, and around the mangrove creeks of Oleta River State Park in North Miami Beach. That is the closest paddle-out option to Miami, and it is a beautiful morning even if you do not see one. Sightings are not guaranteed, and they should never be chased.

The park is Florida’s largest urban state park, about 30 minutes from downtown Miami, open 8 a.m. to sunset year-round. The entry fee is $6 per vehicle; confirm the current fee on the Florida State Parks Oleta River page. It can reach capacity on weekends and then stay closed for the rest of the day, so call the ranger station before a Saturday visit. Kayak and stand-up paddleboard rentals are available through the official concession; check current hours and prices before you go.

Go on a cool, calm morning, paddle slowly near the mangroves, and keep your distance. A widely used rule of thumb is to stay about two kayak lengths away. Do not follow an animal, block its path, or separate a mother and calf. The same advice applies on other quiet edges of the bay.

Crystal River, if you can spare a full day

If you want the version people mean when they say “swim with manatees,” that is a different trip. Crystal River and Kings Bay, on Florida’s Gulf Coast, are about a five-hour drive from Miami. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service manages Crystal River National Wildlife Refuge, and this is the well-known area in Florida where in-water viewing is monitored. Guided kayak and snorkel tours are widely available there in winter.

Even in Kings Bay, the official standard is passive observation: watch calmly from a distance and from the surface. You may not chase a manatee, touch a resting or feeding animal, dive onto one, corner or surround one, ride or hold one, separate a mother and calf, or give a manatee anything to eat or drink. Parts of the bay, including Three Sisters Springs, have additional sanctuary rules, and they are enforced. Book a reputable local guide, go on a cold week, and treat the rules as the point of the day.

Look, but do not touch

This is not a courtesy. It is the law, and it applies everywhere in Florida, not only at Crystal River. The West Indian manatee is protected by the Florida Manatee Sanctuary Act and by federal law, including the Marine Mammal Protection Act and the Endangered Species Act. Florida law makes it unlawful to annoy, molest, harass or disturb a manatee. Harassment includes any act that alters the animal’s natural behavior, and feeding a wild manatee is treated as harassment.

The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission’s guidelines are simple: look, but do not touch; do not feed manatees or give them water; do not chase them; give them room; never separate a mother and calf; and keep noise and splashing down if one surfaces near you. If you are on a boat, slow down in marked zones and post a lookout in shallow water. A conviction under the state law can mean a fine of up to $500 and up to 60 days in jail; a federal conviction can mean a much larger fine and up to a year in prison. Details are on FWC’s manatee viewing guidelines.

A calm bay day is a different outing

If you are already in Miami, a quiet morning on Biscayne Bay can be a lovely winter outing — skyline, mangroves, a swim if the weather is flat. Manatees sometimes appear in the bay and its canals, the same way dolphins sometimes do. That is a lucky aside, not a plan. Do not book a yacht, or ask a captain, to “find manatees.”

Frequently asked questions

Is January a good time to see manatees near Miami?

January is the right season, but the best days depend on the weather. Manatees move into warm water after a few cold days in a row. Check the Manatee Lagoon live cam the morning you go.

Do I need a tour to see manatees in South Florida?

No. The most reliable nearby option, Manatee Lagoon in West Palm Beach, is a free walk-up viewing deck. You can also paddle at Oleta River State Park in North Miami Beach, where sightings are possible but not guaranteed. Guided in-water trips are concentrated in Crystal River, about a five-hour drive from Miami.

Can I swim with manatees near Miami?

You should not plan on it near Miami. Crystal River and Kings Bay, on Florida’s Gulf Coast, are the well-known area where in-water viewing is monitored, and even there the rule is passive observation: look, do not touch, chase, feed or surround the animals.

Is it legal to touch or feed a manatee in Florida?

No. Manatees are protected by the Florida Manatee Sanctuary Act and by federal law, including the Marine Mammal Protection Act and the Endangered Species Act. It is illegal to touch, chase, feed or otherwise harass a manatee. State penalties can include a fine and jail time; federal penalties are higher.
