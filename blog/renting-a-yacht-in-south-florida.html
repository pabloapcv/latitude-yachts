The Complete Guide to Renting a Yacht in South Florida

Pricing, boat types, the best boarding areas, timing, and etiquette — everything to know before you book your first charter in Miami or Fort Lauderdale.

South Florida has one of the largest charter fleets in the country, which is exactly why booking one for the first time feels harder than it should. Hundreds of boats, a dozen boarding areas, pricing that varies by the hour, the day, and the season — this guide covers what actually matters, in the order you’ll need it.

Captained charters vs. bareboat rentals

Almost everything booked through a South Florida charter fleet is a captained charter: a licensed captain and crew run the boat, and you and your guests are along for the ride. You don’t need a boating license, you don’t need experience on the water, and you’re not responsible for docking, fueling, or navigation — the operator’s crew handles all of it. A smaller slice of the market is bareboat (self-drive) rental, which comes with its own licensing and experience requirements that vary by vessel and operator. If you’re booking through a concierge service, assume captained unless told otherwise — it’s simpler, safer, and the standard for groups.

Choosing the right boat

Boats on a charter fleet generally fall into three categories, and the right one depends on your group size and what you want the day to feel like.

Guest capacity is usually estimated from the vessel’s length, not from an official U.S. Coast Guard passenger certificate — always confirm the exact number your group needs with your concierge or operator before booking, especially for groups near a boat’s upper limit.

How pricing works

Most charters are priced by the hour, in blocks — 4, 6, and 8-hour trips are standard, and what you see listed is usually the weekend rate. A few things move the final number:

  • Day of the week — weekday charters are often discounted compared to weekend rates.
  • Holidays — major holidays (Fourth of July, New Year’s, Memorial Day) typically carry a premium and book out weeks in advance.
  • Fuel — longer routes or faster boats burn more fuel, which may be billed separately from the base rate.
  • Gratuity — crew gratuity is customary and usually not included in the listed price (more on this below).
  • Add-ons — catering, decorations, a photographer, or extra water toys are typically quoted separately.

A reputable concierge or operator confirms the all-in price with you before you commit to a booking — if a number seems too good to be true for the boat and duration, ask what it does and doesn’t include.

Where to board in South Florida

Boarding location affects both the vibe of the day and which boats are realistically available to you. The main hubs:

Miami Beach

The most requested boarding area — board from South Beach or Sunset Harbour and you’re minutes from Star Island, Fisher Island, and open views of the downtown skyline. Good for first-time charters and anyone staying on the beach. See the full Miami Beach fleet.

Fort Lauderdale / Broward

Known as the “Yachting Capital of the World” for a reason — the fleet here skews larger, with more motor yachts and superyachts than anywhere else in the region. Boarding is typically along the New River or Las Olas, with quick access to open water through Port Everglades.

Key Biscayne / Rickenbacker

A quieter, more local boarding spot with easy access to sandbars and Stiltsville. A favorite for day cruisers and smaller groups who want less boat traffic.

Downtown Miami / Brickell

Convenient if you’re staying or working in the city center — dock minutes from downtown and Brickell, with fast access out to Biscayne Bay. See the full Brickell & Downtown fleet.

When to book

South Florida charters year-round, but two things affect timing:

  • Season — November through April is peak dry season with the most consistent weather, which also means it’s the most in-demand (and most expensive) stretch. Summer brings more frequent afternoon storms but also lower rates and thinner crowds on the water.
  • Lead time — for a standard weekend charter, 1–2 weeks’ notice is usually enough. For holidays, larger yachts, or group events over 15 people, book 3–4 weeks out if you can — the best boats for a given date go first.

What to expect on the day

  • Arrival — plan to arrive 15 minutes before your departure time to load in and get a safety briefing from the crew.
  • What to bring — sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses, a swimsuit, a change of clothes, and soft-soled or no shoes (most crews ask you to go barefoot or wear boat shoes to protect the deck).
  • Food and drink — policies vary by operator; some allow you to bring your own food and coolers, others offer catering as an add-on. Confirm this when you book, not when you arrive.
  • Seasickness — if you’re prone to it, take precautions before boarding — once underway, options are limited.

Gratuity, in plain terms

Crew gratuity is customary on a captained charter and is almost never included in the listed rate. A typical range is 15–20% of the charter cost, split among the captain and crew, paid at the end of the trip. If you’re unsure what’s expected for your specific charter, ask your concierge before the day of — it’s a normal question, not an awkward one.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a boating license?

No — on a captained charter, the licensed captain operates the vessel. You and your guests are passengers.

How many people can fit on the boat?

It depends on the vessel’s size and the operator’s safety limits, not just how many seats are visible in photos. Confirm your exact party size with your concierge before booking.

What happens if the weather turns?

Operators typically reschedule or refund charters canceled for safety reasons (lightning, small craft advisories, named storms). Exact policies vary by operator — ask what the weather policy is before you pay a deposit.

Can I bring my own alcohol?

Often yes, but policies vary by vessel and operator — confirm before the day.

Is a deposit required?

Most operators require a deposit to hold your date, with the balance due before or on the day of the charter. Your concierge will confirm the exact terms for your specific vessel.
