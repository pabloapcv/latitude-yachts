4, 6 or 8-hour yacht rental

Four hours is the standard minimum and enough for a sunset skyline loop or a focused sandbar stop. Longer blocks cost more in total but less per hour: on our listings a 6-hour charter runs a median about 31% above the 4-hour rate, and 8 hours about 67% more — so you nearly double the time for well under double the price.

Choose 6 or 8 hours when you want lunch at anchor, a Haulover stop and a photo cruise without watching the clock. Ask about weekday rates if your dates are flexible; listed prices are typically weekend rates.

What is usually included

  • Yacht, licensed captain and crew for the booked duration.
  • Fuel for a standard Biscayne Bay cruising route on most boats.
  • Basic safety equipment and a pre-departure briefing.

Plan separately for a 15–20% crew gratuity, catering or BYO food and drinks, decorations, and any long coastal run that burns extra fuel. Marina parking rules vary by boarding area. Your quote lists each line so boats are comparable.

Good to know

Guest counts on listings are editorial estimates based on vessel length. The operator’s licensed limit — confirmed by your concierge — is what counts on the day.

Where Miami yacht rentals depart

Boarding area shapes both your commute and how quickly you reach the sandbar or skyline. Latitude25 covers the main South Florida hubs:

  • Miami Beach — largest fleet; closest to Star Island, Fisher Island and Government Cut.
  • Brickell & Downtown Miami — convenient for mainland hotels; strong mid-size value on the river and bay.
  • Aventura, North Miami Beach, Sunny Isles & Bal Harbour — shortest run to Haulover Sandbar for guests staying north.
  • Fort Lauderdale — Intracoastal and Las Olas cruising; ideal if you are already in Broward.
  • Boca Raton — Palm Beach County boarding for Lake Boca and the Intracoastal.

Key Biscayne and Coconut Grove / Coral Gables also appear in the wider fleet when a route or hotel location calls for them. Your concierge confirms the exact marina with the vessel — it varies by boat, not by a fixed public dock list.

Popular Miami charter routes

Star Island, Fisher Island and the skyline is the classic half-day: mansions, Government Cut and skyline photos without leaving protected water. Captains usually time the loop so you get both the island waterfront and a clean angle on downtown glass. It is the route most first-time visitors picture when they say they want a “Miami yacht day,” and it works well in four hours if you skip a long swim stop.

Haulover Sandbar is the social swim stop — music, other boats, shallow water — especially easy from North Miami and Aventura boarding. Groups that care more about floating than sightseeing often choose this first and add a short skyline pass on the way home if the clock allows. On busy weekends the sandbar fills early; a morning start beats the afternoon crowd.

Key Biscayne offers calmer coves and a more relaxed swim, with less party energy than Haulover and more room to spread out floating mats. Open-ocean runs are possible on suitable boats and longer charters; tell your concierge if that is the goal so the captain and fuel plan match. Combining two of these ideas usually wants a six-hour block so you are not choosing between photos and swimming.

When to rent a yacht in Miami

November through April is peak season: dry, breezy afternoons and high demand on weekends. Hotel inventory fills for Art Basel week and the winter holidays, and the best evening charter slots go with them. Book early for Saturdays, Art Basel week and New Year’s Eve — for NYE, many operators set five- or six-hour minimums and a holiday rate above the standard listing.

May through early November brings warmer water and more weekday availability. Summer water temperatures are ideal for sandbar swimming, but afternoon thunderstorms are common from June through September. Morning departures give you the calmest window and the cleanest weather policy. Shoulder weeks in May and October often feel like peak season without peak crowding. Holiday weekends and major events can still set longer minimum charter lengths — your written quote states the rules for that date.

If your travel dates are fixed but your boarding area is not, ask the concierge to check Miami Beach, Brickell and Aventura in one pass. That single flexibility unlocks more of the 273-vessel fleet than shopping one neighborhood alone, and it often surfaces a better-priced boat for the same guest count.

Tips for first-time Miami yacht rentals

  • Settle the guest list first. Capacity drives the smallest boat that works — and the per-person cost.
  • Shortlist two or three boats in the same size band so you can compare quotes fairly.
  • Ask about glass, red wine and glitter rules before you buy decorations.
  • Bring soft coolers and cans; most operators ban glass bottles.
  • Budget gratuity at 15–20% of the charter rate in cash or Venmo for the crew.
  • Confirm parking and boarding time the day before; marinas differ.
  • Read the weather policy before you pay a deposit, especially in summer.

Frequently asked

How much does yacht rental in Miami cost?

Across 273 listings, 4-hour captained charters start at $520 for a small day boat. A typical 50–59 foot motor yacht sits around $1,720 for 4 hours. Budget 15–20% for crew gratuity on top.

Is a captain included with a Miami yacht rental?

Yes. Every charter Latitude25 arranges is captained by a licensed crew. Guests do not need a boating license.

What is included in the listed rate?

Listed rates typically include the yacht, captain and crew for 4, 6 or 8 hours. Crew gratuity, catering and some long-run fuel charges are usually separate and confirmed in your written quote.

Where do Miami yacht rentals board?

Most board from Miami Beach, Downtown / Brickell, or North Miami / Aventura. Fort Lauderdale and Boca Raton options are available for guests staying farther north.

How far in advance should I book?

For peak-season weekends from November through April, request 1–2 weeks ahead (longer for holidays). Weekdays and summer dates are often available with a few days’ notice.

Is 4, 6 or 8 hours better?

Four hours suits a sunset cruise. Six or eight hours are better value per hour for sandbar days: the median 6-hour rate is about 31% above 4 hours, and 8 hours about 67% more.

Can I rent a yacht for a bachelorette or birthday?

Yes. Latitude25 regularly arranges bachelorette, birthday, proposal, corporate, Art Basel and New Year’s Eve charters across the Miami fleet.
